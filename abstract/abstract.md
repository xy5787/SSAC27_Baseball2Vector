Baseball2Vector: Interpretable Five-Tool Profiles with Predictive Utility

Introduction
WAR and wRC+ summarize how much a hitter produces, not how. Equally valuable hitters can differ sharply in power, contact, plate discipline, defense, and speed. Baseball2Vector (B2V) combines 22 public statistics into these five tools on the 20–80 scouting scale, with input contributions additively traceable before clipping. We test whether B2V (1) distinguishes hitters with similar WAR and wRC+; (2) predicts next season better than simpler summaries, and at what cost versus all inputs and alternative encodings; and (3) adds information to history-based projections, including whether Defense and Speed add value beyond offense.

Methods
FanGraphs data from 2019–2025 yielded 3,070 hitter-seasons with ≥100 plate appearances (PA). B2V shrinks inputs by PA, standardizes and orients them within season, averages equally within tools, and rescales each season's cohort to 50 + 10z, clipped to 20–80. For (1), each of 1,777 focal seasons (626 hitters) was matched to the nearest other same-season hitter within 0.2 WAR and five wRC+ points; standardized root-mean-square profile distances were compared with the hitter's next season and PA-stratified random hitters. For (2)–(3), five expanding-window forecasts (2021–2025) evaluated 1,706 adjacent-season transitions, using ridge regression for next-season wRC+ (primary) and WAR per 600 PA (WAR/600). Comparators were calibrated current performance; fixed (ISO, Contact%, BB%, BsR, Def) and training-selected representative statistics; all 22 inputs; principal component analysis (PCA) with one component per tool or five unrestricted components; and recalibrated Marcel-style projections, alone or supplemented with B2V, representatives, or all inputs. Fitting and selection used training folds only; paired player-cluster bootstrap gave 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Sensitivity analyses excluded 2020-related transitions.

Results
Matched-pair median profile distance was 1.07, versus 1.26 for random pairs and 0.73 for same-hitter pairs: 15.1% below the random-pair median (95% CI, 12.6–17.5%; Figure 1).

For wRC+, B2V reduced mean absolute error (MAE) by 0.92 (0.58–1.26; 4.6%) versus current performance, 0.75 (0.45–1.06) versus fixed representatives, and 0.66 (0.39–0.92) versus training-selected representatives. All three comparisons favored B2V every forecast year (Table 1). Compressing all 22 inputs into five tools raised MAE by 0.24 (0.05–0.43; ≈1.3%). B2V did not differ clearly from either PCA alternative; per-tool PCA reduced MAE versus unrestricted PCA by 0.11 (0.04–0.19), with gains every year, supporting the predictive usefulness of preserving tool groups.

Adding B2V to history-based projections reduced MAE in every forecast year, by 0.37 (0.17–0.57) for wRC+. Adding training-selected representatives instead yielded 0.24 lower MAE (0.06–0.43), a difference inconclusive without 2020-related transitions. For WAR/600, Defense and Speed jointly reduced MAE by 0.121 (0.086–0.155) beyond the offensive tools, again in every year.

Conclusion
B2V compresses 22 statistics into five interpretable dimensions with ≈1.3% higher wRC+ MAE than the full-input model. It distinguishes similarly valued hitters, improves on simpler predictive summaries, and supplements history-based projections. Its compact, traceable profiles could help analysts and front offices compare hitters and communicate statistical strengths and weaknesses. Findings cover hitters with ≥100 PA in consecutive seasons; correspondence with scouts' grades remains untested.
